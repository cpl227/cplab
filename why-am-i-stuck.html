Feeling stuck?

Different kinds of stuck need different ways out. This sorts out which one you're in, then works from there.

Hey, it's Bean from the Lab 👋

What this is: this sorts out which of four kinds of stuck you're actually in, fear, fog, freeze, or a real practical wall, then gives you a next step built for that specific one.

Why it works: research on stuck feelings shows the fix genuinely depends on the type. Treating a fear-based freeze the same as a foggy "I don't know what I want" rarely helps, they need different responses.

What you'll get: pick the type that fits, answer a couple quick questions, get a concrete next step.

Step One · Let's Identify the Type

Tap each one to read more. Land on whichever fits, then tap Next.

Fear-Based

Picking wrong has started to feel dangerous, so you're avoiding the choice altogether. It shows up most with big, hard-to-undo decisions, careers, relationships, moves.

This might look like
  • Rewriting the same email five times and still not sending it
  • Asking everyone you know for their opinion, hoping someone else just decides for you
  • Knowing every pro and con by heart, and somehow no closer to picking

Foggy / Unclear

You don't actually know what you want yet, so there's nothing concrete to move toward. It shows up as everything feeling like a maybe, and nothing feeling like a yes.

This might look like
  • Someone asks what you actually want, and you genuinely don't have an answer
  • You've tried writing out goals and it just feels hollow
  • Everything feels like a maybe, nothing feels like a yes

Frozen / Overwhelmed

This one's physiological, not motivational, your body has hit a wired or shut-down state. It gets mistaken for laziness, but it isn't that.

This might look like
  • You know exactly what to do, and still can't make yourself start
  • Even small tasks feel like they take more energy than you have
  • It's been called laziness, maybe even by you, but it feels more like being stuck than not caring

Practical

A real external constraint is in the way, money, time, or support, not a mindset problem. It doesn't need motivation, it needs the missing piece.

This might look like
  • You know exactly what you'd do with $500 more, or 5 more hours in a day
  • The plan is solid, the resource to actually run it isn't there yet
  • People keep telling you to "just" do the thing that needs something you don't have access to

About the research
  • Schwartz, B. (2002). Maximizing versus satisficing: Happiness is a matter of choice. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 83(5), 1178–1197, choosing "good enough" over "the best possible."
  • Neipp, M-C., et al. (2021). Solution-focused versus problem-focused questions: Differential effects of miracles, exceptions and scales. Journal of Family Therapy, miracle and exception questions reduce negative affect; scaling questions generate more concrete action steps.
  • Psychology Today (2023). Feeling stuck? Two types of procrastination, the wired (sympathetic) vs. shut-down (parasympathetic) freeze response.
  • D'Zurilla, T. J., & Goldfried, M. R. Structured problem-solving research, breaking a real constraint into its smallest solvable piece.
Before you go This is a self-reflection tool, not a diagnostic one, and it can't tell you which kind of stuck you're "really" in, only which one you recognize. If this stuck feeling has lasted a long time, or comes with hopelessness that scares you, that's worth bringing to a person, not just a page. If you're in crisis or having thoughts of self-harm, please contact a crisis line or emergency services in your area.
